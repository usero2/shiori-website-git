Your Library, on Every Device
Cloud Sync with Google Drive, End to End

☁️

You highlight a paragraph on the train, rate a book before bed, and get halfway through chapter nine on your tablet. Then the phone dies, or a new one arrives, and every one of those small acts of reading is gone. Cloud Sync is Shiori's answer: your books, your place in them, your highlights, notes, ratings and reading statistics, all kept in your own Google Drive.

Not on our server. Shiori has no server. This guide walks through every part of it — signing in, what each switch actually moves, how book files differ from everything else, what happens on a second device, and which rule wins when two devices disagree.

The one-sentence version: sign in with Google, leave Auto Sync on, and tap Sync to Cloud on the books you want stored. Everything else in this article is detail you can come back for.

Where Your Data Actually Goes

Shiori asks for exactly two Google Drive permissions — drive.file and drive.appdata — and neither one can read the rest of your Drive. Shiori sees the files it created, and nothing else. Those files land in two different places, and knowing which is which explains most of the app's behaviour.

LocationWhat lives thereCan you see it in Drive?
Shiori Library Your actual book files — EPUB, KEPUB, CBZ, PDF, FB2 Yes — an ordinary folder you can open, browse and back up yourself
Hidden app folder Settings, bookmarks, highlights, notes, ratings, reading statistics, covers, AI chats, saved translations, imported fonts, your Thai TTS dictionary No — Drive's private per-app storage, invisible in the Drive UI

Both count against your Google account's storage quota, and the book files are the only part big enough to notice. A shelf of EPUBs is usually a few hundred megabytes; a comic library in CBZ can be much more.

Worth knowing before you start: only books uploaded from the app appear in Shiori's cloud list. If you drop an EPUB into the "Shiori Library" folder by hand from a computer, the app will not see it — drive.file deliberately does not grant access to files the app did not create.

What You Will Need

  • A Google account — the same one on every device you want to keep in step.
  • Free Drive space — enough for the books you choose to upload. Settings and highlights are tiny.
  • A network connection — Wi-Fi for the first upload of a large library.

Step-by-Step Guide (7 Steps)

1Sign In With Google

Open Settings → Cloud Sync (Google Drive). Before you sign in, the page is a single card: one button, and a line telling you what it is for.

Tap Sign in with Google and pick your account. Shiori stores nothing about you beyond the account handle it needs to talk to Drive — there is no Shiori account, no password, and nothing to create.

2Meet the Cloud Sync Page

Once signed in, the page grows into four parts, top to bottom:

  • Your account, with a real Sign out button beside it.
  • Synced devices — every device backing up to this account (step 7).
  • Everything — the manual backup and restore of all your settings and Memory, plus a repair tool.
  • Auto Sync — the switches that decide what moves on its own.

The red line under Synced devices is not decoration. Multi-device sync is marked experimental, and the warning sits where the decision is made rather than two screens later.

3Back Up Everything, Once

Tap Backup setting. Nothing uploads yet — first you get an inventory of exactly what is about to travel, because a backup button with a one-line caption is a leap of faith.

The list covers reader settings (font, size, theme, line height, margins, page mode), read-aloud settings (engine, languages, voices, pitch and speed, highlight colours, Thai dictionary), Memory (highlights, notes, bookmarks, ratings and AI chats for every book), text tools (replacement rules, translation languages and engines, saved translations), covers and imported fonts, and your library server connections.

It is equally clear about what is not in there: book files, which sync per book from the bookshelf; reading statistics, which sync automatically on their own; and supporter status, which stays with Google Play.

Restore settings opens the same dialog in reverse — worth reading, because a restore overwrites what is on this device.

4Set Auto Sync and Forget It

Manual backups are the safety net; Auto Sync is what you will actually live with. Each switch covers one topic, and switching one off never makes that data unbackupable — only un-automatic, since the manual backup above always includes everything.

The table below the screenshot explains each one.

SwitchWhat it keeps in step

Below the switches sit four buttons — Back up / Restore Visual and Back up / Restore TTS. They move only your replacement rules, separately from the full backup, which is what you want when carrying a carefully tuned pronunciation list to a new phone without touching anything else.

5Send Book Files to the Cloud

Book files are deliberately not automatic. Shiori never quietly uploads a 600 MB comic archive over mobile data — you choose, per book.

Long-press any book on the shelf and tap Sync to Cloud. The upload runs as a background service with a progress notification, so you can keep reading while it works, and it survives leaving the app.

Synced books wear a small cloud badge on their cover, and a Cloud Sync row appears on the bookshelf as a shortcut to everything you have stored.

Private books are exempt by design: anything opened in Incognito never syncs — not the file, not the position, not a highlight.

6Browse What Is in the Cloud

Tap the Cloud Sync row on your bookshelf to open the cloud list. Every stored book shows its chapter count, file size, how far you have read it, the date it was uploaded and its file name — plus an On this device badge when a local copy exists.

From here you can Read a book you already have, Download one you do not, or search the whole cloud library by title. Long-press gives you the rest: Remove from cloud, Remove from device (they are separate, and the app says so), and Select for multi-select clean-ups.

Downloading restores more than the file. Bookmarks, highlights and your reading position come down with it, so a book arrives on the new phone exactly where you left it on the old one.

7Add a Second Device

On the new phone or tablet, sign in to the same Google account. Content that syncs both ways — highlights, notes, ratings, reading position, statistics, shelves — arrives on its own. Then open Synced devices.

Every device that has ever backed up to this account is listed with its model, app version and last sync time. Settings are kept per device, on purpose: two devices auto-syncing into one shared settings file used to overwrite each other's preferences on every push, and a phone and a tablet rarely want the same font size anyway.

When you do want them identical, tap Restore here on the device whose setup you like and it is copied onto the one in your hand. The trash icon removes a device from the list, with an optional tick to delete its settings backup as well.

Which Way Does Each Thing Travel?

"It syncs" is not one behaviour. Some things flow both ways continuously, some only go up until you ask for them back, and a couple never leave the device at all. This is the honest map.

The Sync Now Button

The cloud icon in the bookshelf's top bar is a full round trip, not just an upload: it pushes this device's settings, then pulls down statistics, per-book facts, shelves and reading queue from the others. It spins while it works and stays quiet when it finishes — the button you press in passing should not open a dialog every time.

When Something Looks Wrong

  • "No settings found in Drive" — nothing has been backed up to this account yet. Run Backup setting on the device that has the setup you want first.
  • A book syncs on one device but never updates on the other — their file names have drifted apart. Everything → Repair Drive links → Check for problems looks without changing anything and shows you every rename it would make before you agree to it.
  • Books you added to the Drive folder by hand are missing — expected. Import them into the app and use Sync to Cloud, and they become visible to Shiori.
  • You cancelled a sync — some items may already have moved. Running it again finishes the job; every operation is safe to repeat.
  • Signing out — clears the account from this device only. Nothing in Drive is deleted, and signing back in picks up where you left off.

A Note on Privacy

There is no Shiori account, no Shiori server, and no analytics attached to any of this. Your books and highlights go from your phone to your Drive and back. We cannot read them, and neither can the app outside the files it created. Incognito books are excluded entirely, and if you ever want out, sign out and delete the "Shiori Library" folder — that plus the hidden app folder is all there ever was.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Shiori upload all my books automatically?
No. Book files are uploaded one at a time, when you long-press a book and choose Sync to Cloud. Everything smaller — settings, highlights, notes, ratings, reading position and statistics — can sync automatically, and does by default.
Where are my files inside Google Drive?
Book files sit in a normal, visible folder called Shiori Library. Everything else lives in Drive's hidden per-app folder, which does not appear in the Drive interface. Both belong to your account and count towards its storage.
Can Shiori read the rest of my Google Drive?
No. It asks only for the drive.file and drive.appdata permissions, which limit an app to files it created itself. That is also why a book you copy into the Shiori Library folder by hand stays invisible to the app.
Will my reading position follow me to another device?
Yes, for books that are synced, with Auto-sync reading position on. The position is written when you leave the reader, and the most recently read device wins if two disagree.
Why do my two devices have different font sizes after a restore?
Because reader and TTS settings are kept per device on purpose — a phone and a tablet rarely want the same layout. To copy one setup onto another, open Synced devices and tap Restore here.
What happens to my Drive data when I sign out?
Nothing. Signing out only disconnects this device. Your backup stays in Drive, and signing back in — on this device or a new one — reconnects to it.
Are books I read in Incognito mode synced?
Never. Incognito books are excluded from every sync path: the file, the reading position, bookmarks and statistics all stay on the device.
Is using two devices safe?
It works, and it is marked experimental in the app for a reason: everything that auto-syncs — notes, bookmarks, highlights, statistics — is merged by rules, and rules can surprise you. Keep the warning in mind, and use Repair Drive links if a book stops updating on one side.
Ready to try it? Get Shiori on Google Play, then open Settings → Cloud Sync (Google Drive) and sign in. The first backup takes seconds.
